Got a Letter from the Estonian Police? A Calm Step-by-Step Guide

Advocat AI Legal Team 16 July 2026 11 min read

An envelope from the Politsei- ja Piirivalveamet — Estonia's Police and Border Guard Board — makes your pulse jump even when you have done nothing wrong. Here is the first thing worth knowing: a PPA letter does not mean you are accused of anything. The PPA is Estonia's police force, border guard and immigration authority rolled into one agency. The same letterhead appears on a witness summons, a speed-camera fine and a routine letter about your residence permit.

The second thing worth knowing: almost every such letter contains a deadline, and it is the deadline — not the letter's tone — that decides how fast you need to act. Let's go through it calmly.

Step 1: find the role you are addressed in

Estonian law has three main types of proceedings, each with its own rules. The opening lines of the letter — and the statute it cites — usually tell you which one you are dealing with:

  • Criminal proceedings (kriminaalmenetlus) — the letter cites the Code of Criminal Procedure (KrMS) and you are summoned as a witness (tunnistaja), victim (kannatanu) or suspect (kahtlustatav). Your role must be stated on the summons (KrMS § 163).
  • Misdemeanour proceedings (väärteomenetlus) — the letter cites the Code of Misdemeanour Procedure (VTMS). Traffic offences, public order violations, petty theft — and speed-camera notices.
  • Administrative proceedings (haldusmenetlus) — residence permits, visas, documents, orders. References to the Aliens Act (VMS) or the Administrative Procedure Act (HMS). Nobody is accusing you of anything here, but the deadlines tend to be the strictest.

Sergei, who lives in Tallinn's Lasnamäe district, got a letter headed "Kutse" last month and was convinced he was under suspicion. In fact, the third paragraph said he was invited tunnistajana — as a witness to a car accident outside his building. Those are two very different situations, which is why the role is the first thing to look for.

Step 2: check the letter is genuine

Scammers send letters in the police's name too. A genuine PPA letter arrives on paper, from an official e-mail address (ending in @politsei.ee) or through the state portal eesti.ee. It names the official handling your matter, gives their phone number and a case reference.

Signs of a scam: an immediate demand for money, a payment link leading to an unfamiliar site, threats of "arrest within 24 hours", an odd sender address. The Estonian police never ask you to pay a fine through a link in a letter. When in doubt, call the PPA information line +372 612 3000 and ask whether the letter was really sent to you.

Step 3: find the deadline and write it down today

Scan the letter for "hiljemalt" (no later than), "tähtaeg" (deadline), "… jooksul" (within …) or a specific date and time. Put it in your calendar the day you open the letter. A large share of the problems lawyers untangle later began with nothing more dramatic than a missed date.

What happens if you miss it depends on the type of proceeding:

  • In criminal proceedings, ignoring a summons can lead to a penalty payment (KrMS § 138) or to being brought in by the police (KrMS § 139).
  • In a misdemeanour case, the decision takes effect and your chance to appeal disappears.
  • In an administrative case (say, a residence permit), the PPA decides on the file as it stands — missing documents simply count against you.

If it is a summons to an interview

As a witness

A witness summons means the police believe you know something useful. You need to attend, and you need to tell the truth. Bring an ID document and the summons itself. If the time does not suit you — work, travel — call the number on the summons before the date and agree on a new time. It is an entirely ordinary request.

As a suspect

A more serious situation, but the same principle applies: staying calm is your best tool. A suspect has rights guaranteed by law (KrMS § 34); the three that matter most:

  • the right to know what you are suspected of — this must be explained to you before the interview;
  • the right to counsel — you may insist that the interview take place with a lawyer present. If you cannot afford one, state legal aid is available in certain cases;
  • the right to remain silent — you are not obliged to testify against yourself.
Practical advice: as a suspect, do not walk into a first interview unprepared. Discuss with your lawyer beforehand what you will and will not say. Whatever goes into the interview record stays in the case file, and it is very hard to "take back" later.

If it is a fine or misdemeanour decision

A speed-camera notice, a parking violation, riding without a ticket — these fall under misdemeanour procedure. Two scenarios:

  • You agree: pay the fine by the date on the notice. That closes the matter.
  • You disagree: a misdemeanour decision (väärteootsus) can be appealed to the county court within 15 days of receipt (VTMS § 114). For speed-camera notices, the contesting procedure is printed on the notice itself — relevant, for instance, if someone else was driving your car.

Before appealing, weigh honestly whether you have an argument: wrong vehicle identified, a different driver, a factual error in the record, unmarked roadworks. "The fine feels too high" rarely succeeds on its own, though your financial situation can be taken into account when the official chooses between a warning and a fine.

If the letter concerns your residence permit or documents

Because the PPA is also the immigration authority, a large share of the letters foreigners receive comes from this side of the house: a request for additional documents, notice that the review period has been extended, an invitation to an interview or — worst case — a refusal or revocation of a residence permit.

  • Request for documents: reply by the deadline even if you do not yet have everything. For missing items, say when you will provide them, and if needed ask in writing for an extension before the deadline passes.
  • Negative decision: you can choose between an objection (vaie) to the PPA within 30 days (HMS § 75) or a complaint to the administrative court within 30 days of notification (HKMS § 46). The court can also suspend the decision's enforcement while your case is heard (preliminary legal protection).

Removal orders and orders to leave are a big topic of their own — we cover them in a separate guide: how to challenge a removal order in Estonia.

If Estonian is not your language

In criminal proceedings the position is clear: if you do not speak Estonian, an interpreter is arranged for you (KrMS § 10). Say so as soon as you receive the summons — the interpreter should be present at the interview, not only later in court.

In administrative proceedings you are worse off: the process runs in Estonian, and translation is usually your own problem. This is where most mistakes happen — someone replies using machine translation, submits half of what was requested, and receives a refusal two months later. If a letter is unclear, have it checked before you answer by someone who knows both the language and the bureaucratic register.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to show up if the Estonian police summon me?

Generally yes. In criminal proceedings, failing to appear without good reason can lead to a penalty payment (KrMS § 138) or to being brought in by the police (KrMS § 139). If the time does not work, call the number on the summons before the date and ask to reschedule.

How do I know whether I am summoned as a witness or a suspect?

The summons (kutse) must state your procedural role — KrMS § 163 requires it. Look for "tunnistajana" (as a witness) or "kahtlustatavana" (as a suspect). If it is unclear, ask the investigator in writing before the interview.

How long do I have to contest a fine?

A misdemeanour decision can be appealed to the county court within 15 days of receipt (VTMS § 114). For speed-camera notices, the procedure and deadline are printed on the notice. Miss the deadline and the decision takes effect.

The PPA asked for extra documents for my residence permit. What if I miss the deadline?

The PPA decides on the file as it stands — often negatively when documents are missing. Ask for an extension in writing before the deadline. A refusal can be challenged by objection within 30 days (HMS § 75) or in the administrative court within 30 days (HKMS § 46).

I don't speak Estonian. Am I entitled to an interpreter?

In criminal proceedings, yes (KrMS § 10). In administrative matters the process runs in Estonian and translation is usually your own responsibility — one more reason to have your reply checked before sending.
